    02 · Activity

    Screen time

    Where your time actually goes — measured on this device, and kept here.

    Measurement Off

    Off until you turn it on. Sieve then keeps a local tally of how long you spend on which site — no page content, no addresses beyond the site name, nothing that leaves this browser.

    Measure my screen time Time per site and per hour, so the report below has something to show. Only the tab you are looking at counts, and the clock stops when you switch to another app or lock the screen.

    Anything older than that is deleted automatically. Extension pages, the new-tab page, local files and private windows are never counted.

    0m

    Nothing measured yet. Browse for a few minutes and the shape of your day appears here.

    Reading a page counts as time on it. Sieve stops the clock after a few minutes with no keyboard or mouse activity, so a tab left open while you are away is not counted as attention.

    Games

    Curated lists that ship with Sieve. Browser only: Sieve can block game sites and store pages, but not games already installed on this computer, or anything on a console.

    Block browser game portals Play-in-browser sites — Poki, CrazyGames, CoolMathGames, Y8, Miniclip, Kongregate — plus the big standalone .io games, and the shared host most portals serve from, so embedded games stop loading too.
    Block game download stores Steam, Epic, GOG, itch.io, Humble, console storefronts, key resellers and Android emulators — so no new games get browsed or bought. An installed game keeps working.
    Block game platforms & online worlds The web side of Roblox, Minecraft, Fortnite, Riot, Habbo and similar. Websites and web players are blocked; installed desktop clients are out of an extension’s reach.
    Block game streaming, cloud gaming & esports Twitch, Kick, now.gg, Boosteroid, plus esports stats and ranking sites — for less time around games, not just less time playing them.

    AI tools

    These block the AI tools themselves, for your own focus. Not to be confused with AI content farms under Safety, which blocks sites whose pages are AI-generated spam.

    Block AI chatbots & assistants General-purpose AI chat and AI search — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, DeepSeek, Grok, Poe — plus coding chats used in a browser tab. The chat sites themselves, not a vendor’s API, so sites that quietly use AI keep working; an assistant built into your editor is out of reach either way.
    Block AI writing, homework & humanizer tools AI writing and paraphrasing tools, homework helpers, and the “humanizer” sites sold to get AI text past a detector — QuillBot, Jenni, Caktus, Undetectable, StealthGPT. Grammar checkers and human essay sites are deliberately left alone.
    Block AI companions & roleplay chat Character.AI, Replika, Janitor AI, Chai and similar — the ones built to be talked to for hours. Explicit sites are outside what this covers, as with the dating list.

    Two limits worth knowing: a search engine’s own AI answers cannot be blocked without blocking the search engine, and image, video and voice generators are not covered. Add either to Blocked sites if you want them gone.

    Blocked sites

    Your own list. Anything here is always blocked, whichever blockers are on — including as a source of images on other sites.

    A site, or wildcard
    pornhub.com, *.inappropriate.net, example.com/adult/*
    A whole top-level domain
    .xyz or xyz blocks every site ending in it — the broadest entry you can write, so check it is what you want. Images from a blocked TLD are hidden page-side rather than by a network rule.
    Pattern on the address
    /example\.(net|org)/
    Pattern on the page title
    title/Example Domain/ — matched once the page loads, so it appears briefly first.
    A note
    A line starting with # or ! is ignored when matching, and stays above the entries under it — use it as a heading.

    Patterns are checked as you save; one that is invalid, or slow enough to freeze pages, is refused with the reason.

      Allowlist

      Sites here are never blocked, even when a list includes them. It applies to every blocker, and to ad & tracker blocking.

      Plain domains only — example.com covers its subdomains. Wildcards, patterns and notes belong to Blocked sites: an allow rule has to be certain which site it is opening up.

        04 · Filters

        Ads & trackers

        Block ads, and the companies that follow you from site to site.

        Ad & tracker blocker Beta

        One switch for all of it — ad and tracking domains, YouTube video ads, Facebook sponsored posts, and the “turn off your ad blocker” walls.

        Block ads & trackers Blocks ad and tracking domains, skips YouTube ads, hides Facebook sponsored posts, clears “disable your ad blocker” messages, and tidies up the empty gaps left behind. Domain lists derived from EasyList and EasyPrivacy — © The EasyList authors, used under CC BY-SA 3.0.

        If a site breaks, add it to your Allowlist — that turns this off for that one site and leaves it on everywhere else. Please also report it.

        On Firefox this counter stays at zero. Firefox will not let an extension read back which of its block rules fired. The blocking itself is unaffected.

        What it can and cannot do

        Most of it is domain blocking. Sieve stops the browser talking to known ad and tracking services.

        The empty boxes get tidied up too. Blocking an advert stops the advert but leaves the space it was going to sit in, so a page can end up full of gaps. Sieve hides those boxes once it is sure they are staying empty. It waits a few seconds first, on purpose: hiding an advert's box immediately is one of the ways a site works out you are using a blocker.

        It cannot remove an advert that did load — one a site serves from its own address, or its own promotions for sister publications. Those need a different kind of filtering that Sieve does not do yet.

        It tries to get past “disable your ad blocker” walls. Most sites work this out by loading an advert and checking whether it arrived, so Sieve answers those checks instead of leaving them to fail. If a message appears anyway it is cleared, and the scroll lock and the blur that usually come with it are undone. A site that decides on its own server, or that refuses to send the page at all, cannot be helped this way.

        YouTube and Facebook work differently. Both serve ads from the same address as the content itself, so there is no address to block that would not also block the video or the feed. Sieve edits those out of the page data as it arrives instead.

        On YouTube itself, the domain blocking stands down. There the ad and tracking addresses are Google's, and Google is the site, so blocking them protects you from nothing it cannot already see. What it does do is stop YouTube's ads reporting that they were seen — which is exactly what YouTube looks for when it decides you are using a blocker. YouTube's ads are dealt with inside the page instead.

        If YouTube notices, Sieve asks again. YouTube can tell when an ad it scheduled never played, and it answers by holding the video back or refusing to play it. Sieve then has the player load the same video again, from where you were, asking for it in a way YouTube does not put ads in front of. There are a few such ways and YouTube closes them now and then, so Sieve tries them in turn and keeps the one that works. You may see the video restart or the player flicker for a moment while it does.

        Only if every way is refused does it fast-forward. Then Sieve stops removing video ads on this browser for a while and fast-forwards them instead — muted, at 16× — so YouTube sees them played and keeps your videos playing. After a cool-down it goes back to removing them.

        It can hide the wrong thing. The Facebook part and the wall remover both read the page rather than blocking an address, so a real post — or a real pop-up — can occasionally be hidden. Nothing is deleted: switch this off, reload, and it comes straight back.

        Expect it to break. YouTube and Facebook change how ads are marked and delivered without warning, often faster than an extension update can be reviewed and published. When that happens the ads simply come back until Sieve is updated; nothing stops working.

        Some YouTube ads cannot be removed by any extension. YouTube is moving to stitching the ad into the video stream itself, so refusing the ad means refusing the video. Those come and go depending on the session.

        Sites that break tend to break at sign-in or checkout. Some run their bot and fraud checks through the same companies that do the tracking. Allowlist the site and it will work again.

        It leaves “Suggested for you”, “People you may know” and the Reels row alone — that is Facebook promoting Facebook, not advertising.

        Every blocked request is counted in the Overview. Expect big numbers: one page can pull in dozens of trackers. Only the running total is kept — never which site, never which tracker.

        Floating videos Beta

        Stop the video at the top of an article shrinking into a corner and riding down the page with you.

        Stop videos following you Puts a floating player back where the site first put it — still there, still playing if it was, just no longer in the way.

        If a site breaks, add it to your Allowlist — that turns this off for that one site and leaves it on everywhere else.

        What it can and cannot do

        It moves the player, it does not remove it. The video goes back where the site first put it, still playing if it was. That matters on the pages where the video is the thing you came for — hiding it would leave a hole instead.

        It also does not press the player's own close button for you. That would tidy up a little better on the players that have one, but it would change the site's own state, and then switching this off and reloading would no longer give you the page exactly as the site sent it. Nothing here is deleted or clicked.

        Sites where a floating video is the whole point are left alone — YouTube, Twitch, Netflix and the rest. So is every video call: a meeting cannot lose the picture of the person talking, on the listed services or on any other, because Sieve can tell a live camera from a recording. Picture-in-picture is left alone too, because that is you asking for it.

        It can get the wrong box. It knows the common players by name and finds the rest by shape — a video-sized panel pinned to a corner — so something else pinned to a corner can occasionally be moved as well. Switch this off and reload and it goes back.

        This is not ad blocking, which is why it has its own switch. The floating player is the site's own, served from the site's own address, so there is no advertising address to block that would not also block the article.

              09 · Controls

              Lock

              Lock your settings against impulse changes — yours, or anyone else’s.

              Guardian Lock Off

              With a PIN set, turning anything off — or getting past the pause screen — asks for it. Strengthening your protection never does. With no PIN, Sieve is under your own control, apart from any cool-off you set below.

              Set a PIN

              Four characters or more — numbers, letters, or whole sentences.

              Change or remove your PIN

              Require an access code A second step after your PIN: a random code appears and you retype it by hand — copy and paste are switched off, and a typo gives you a fresh one. It isn’t a secret; the point is the minute of deliberate effort, which is usually long enough for an urge to pass. Turning it off again also needs the code.
              Ask on every change Not just the decisive ones. Left off, the code guards turning a protection off, getting past the pause screen, allowing a blocked site, or weakening the code itself — small edits need only your PIN.

              Cool-off Off

              A PIN you know is no lock in a weak moment. A wait is. With a cool-off set, anything that weakens your protection — switching a blocker off, allowing a site, removing your PIN — is asked for first, and unlocks only once the wait is over. Strengthening is still instant. Works with or without a PIN.

              Nothing turns off on its own. Once the wait is over, that one change stays unlocked for a day: come back and make it yourself, or let it lapse. Shortening the wait or turning it off has to wait it out too. The pause screen’s “15 more minutes” isn’t covered — it is a daily limit, and a wait would outlast it.

                About

                Why I made Sieve

                Hey — thanks for installing Sieve.

                Honestly, I made this because I got tired. The internet I grew up on felt fun and open. Somewhere over the years it turned into scams, nasty comments, endless feeds, and sites that trick you at every click. It stopped feeling safe or clean — for kids, for grown-ups, for pretty much anyone.

                So I built the tool I wished existed: something that quietly cleans all that up and gives you back a bit of control. It all runs on your own device — nothing tracked, no account, no catch. That's it.

                I just want the internet to feel a little more human again. Hope Sieve helps.

                — monolab
